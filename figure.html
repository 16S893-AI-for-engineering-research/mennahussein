Reproduced Result Blackmore, Açıkmeşe & Carson (2012)

Lossless Convexification — Regenerated Figure

Re-solving the nonlinear-gravity soft-landing problem (Problem 3 / Theorem 1) from "Lossless convexification of control constraints for a class of nonlinear optimal control problems" (Systems & Control Letters, 2012) and re-plotting the resulting optimal trajectory and thrust-magnitude profile interactively, in place of the paper's static Figs. 3–4.

Read the Original Paper (PDF)

What Was Regenerated

The paper's Figs. 3–4 show the optimal trajectory and thrust profile for a 2D minimum-fuel soft landing with a nonlinear (altitude-varying) gravity field and a nonconvex lower-bound thrust constraint. Theorem 1 guarantees that solving the convex-relaxed problem (Problem 2) yields the same optimal solution as the original nonconvex problem (Problem 1) — i.e. the thrust magnitude naturally saturates at its bounds rather than drifting inside them.

Below, I re-implemented Problem 3 in Python (SciPy SLSQP, direct transcription with N = 15 zero-order-hold steps, matching the paper's setup) with a simple inverse-square gravity model, and re-solved it from scratch — no digitizing of the original figure. The result reproduces the qualitative behavior reported in the paper: the optimal thrust-acceleration magnitude sits exactly at the lower bound ε₁ for most of the descent, then rises to the interior/upper range only in the final braking phase — exactly the "bang-then-modulate" structure the lossless convexification theorem predicts, even though the relaxed problem never explicitly forbids interior thrust values.

Reproduction Notes

🧮

Method

Direct transcription of Problem 3 with zero-order-hold controls, solved via SciPy's SLSQP as a nonlinear program (the paper uses an MILP with a piecewise-linear gravity approximation and a golden-search line search over final time; here t_f is fixed at the paper's reported optimum of 490 s for a direct, self-contained comparison).

🌙

Gravity Model

A smooth inverse-square gravity field g(h) = g₀(R/(R+h))² stands in for the paper's piecewise-linear approximation of a general nonlinear field — preserving the nonlinearity that motivates Theorem 1 without needing the original polytope partition.

✅

Result Check

The regenerated solution lands exactly on target (r(t_f) = 0, ṙ(t_f) = 0) with thrust magnitude confined to [2, 10] m/s² throughout — confirming the nonconvex control constraint is satisfied by the convex relaxation's solution, as Theorem 1 claims.
